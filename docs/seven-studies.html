1. How often is the forecast range respected?

A strict breakout means any five-minute high above the upper boundary or low below the lower boundary. A touch alone is not a breakout. Each day belongs to exactly one of four categories: never broke, broke then closed inside, stalled outside, or closed beyond the buffer.

“Stalled” uses the final close of that SessionPath window. For a $89–$91 forecast, the 10% outer band is $88.80–$91.20. The 15% outer band is $88.70–$91.30. An inside close is classified separately from an outside stall.

2. Does directional occupancy carry into the next window?

U means a completed close above that window’s actual decision open. D means below. All closes count, including closes beyond the forecast. Unchanged closes stay in the denominator. A window is U-dominant when it has more U closes than D closes; this is different from finishing above its open.

U90 means at least 90% of all closes were U. With 24 candles in 03:00–05:00, that requires at least 22 U closes. D90 is symmetric. In the strength tables, U and D mean dominant but below the 90% threshold.

3. Printed AVR versus subsequent expansion

Printed AVR = (highest high − lowest low from 19:30 to the cutoff) ÷ the fixed 10-day AVR. Additional expansion = the later cumulative range minus the range already printed, divided by the same AVR. A later window can move actively inside the old high–low and add exactly zero.

The main groups are disjoint: below 25%,25–<50%,50–<75%,75–<100%, and 100%+. For “around 25/50/75/100,” the separate landmark tables use ±5 percentage points. Exact floating-point equality would leave unrepresentatively small samples.

Did the added information improve prediction?

For each test day, use only earlier eligible dates. The first 250 dates initialize the model. The baseline predicts the historical frequencies of U, D and TIE. The condition-based model uses the matching group, shrunk toward the baseline by 20 prior-day equivalents. These settings were fixed for this analysis and not optimized.

What is being predicted? Whether the next session has more candle closes above its decision open (U), more below it (D), or equal counts (TIE). The baseline uses all earlier eligible dates without the added condition. The comparison uses earlier dates matching the stated preceding-session direction or strength. Both predict the same outcome on the same test date. This is a direction-probability comparison, not an AVR-expansion test, dollar forecast-error test, BBOX-return test or target-hit test.

The score is the sum of squared probability errors for U, D and TIE, averaged across days. Lower is better. A negative model-minus-baseline difference would indicate improvement. Every displayed point estimate is positive and every 95% block-bootstrap interval spans zero.
