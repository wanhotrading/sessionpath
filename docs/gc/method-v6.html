What these estimates can tell you

These are conditional historical excursions, useful as volatility context. They are not a predicted route, target, win rate or guarantee that either side will trade. An indicator can calculate a chart-based model, but the rejected rolling mean is not the website’s historical matching model. Version 6 uses website imports again.

Two separate windows

At 03:00, use only information available by 03:00 to estimate 03:00–05:00. At 05:00, update the inputs to estimate 05:00–08:30. The second window requires a new code; the indicator does not reuse the first window’s widths. Times are New York local time with daylight saving handled automatically. Normal mode uses today’s New York date; Replay lets you select a historical date.

Historical excursions use the actual decision-time opening price from the archived candle data. Snapshot conditions use earlier completed candles plus that opening price, not the decision candle’s later high, low or close. Missing required observations are excluded. Generate fresh codes for each tested date; repositioning a code onto an earlier chart day does not recalculate its historical matches.

AVR and the 114% question

AVR is a price-point baseline: the mean high−low range of ten prior complete NY 09:30–16:00 sessions on the same delivery contract. If AVR is 2.00 points and the overnight range printed is 114%, the observed overnight range is 1.14 × 2.00 = 2.28 points. A coefficient such as 0.55 must multiply the 2.00-point AVR, not the 114% progress figure.

Remaining U/D from the cutoff price

For historical day i:
uᵢ = max(future high − cutoff price, 0) ÷ AVRᵢ
dᵢ = max(cutoff price − future low, 0) ÷ AVRᵢ
Average U points = mean(uᵢ) × your AVR
Average D points = mean(dᵢ) × your AVR
Price levels = your cutoff price + U, and your cutoff price − D

Example: historical normalized U values 0.20, 0.40 and 0.90, with your AVR 2.00 points. Mean U = 0.50 × 2.00 = 1.00 point; median U = 0.40 × 2.00 = 0.80; maximum U = 0.90 × 2.00 = 1.80. At price 75.00, those upper levels are 76.00, 75.80 and 76.80.

The teal/red box edges show the sample’s mean normalized upside and mean normalized downside, multiplied by your AVR. White dotted lines show medians; orange dotted lines show means at the box edges. Medians may lie outside the mean box. Sample maxima remain reference statistics and do not determine the box size. A mean box is not a confidence interval or a guaranteed range. This change makes the display more representative of average excursions; it does not establish predictive accuracy.

External expansion: outside which box?

eUᵢ = max(future high − high already printed, 0) ÷ AVRᵢ
eDᵢ = max(low already printed − future low, 0) ÷ AVRᵢ
Average extra range = mean(eUᵢ + eDᵢ) × your AVR

This measures expansion beyond the observed running high/low since 19:30. It is not specifically beyond the orange AVR projection. If the current range is 74.00–76.00 and scaled mean expansions are 0.30 above and 0.20 below, the mapped levels are 76.30 and 73.80; projected range size is 2.00 + 0.30 + 0.20 = 2.50 points. Large remaining U/D can occur entirely inside an existing range, so remaining excursion and extra range are different quantities.

Whole overnight totals

Total Uᵢ = (max(19:30 open, highs through endpoint) − 19:30 open) ÷ AVRᵢ
Total Dᵢ = (19:30 open − min(19:30 open, lows through endpoint)) ÷ AVRᵢ
Displayed totals = sample mean of each ratio × your AVR

Your time and entered observations select comparable historical days; your AVR supplies the price-point scale. This does not force U + D to equal today’s AVR. It can be smaller or larger. Whole-night U/D starts from the historical 19:30 open; remaining U/D starts from the cutoff price. “Current range + mean expansion” uses your observed range, so it can differ from the matched sample’s mean total overnight range.

How cases are selected

Only prior dates and complete required five-minute windows qualify. Missing bars are excluded, not filled. Tags must have become known by the selected cutoff. Tags, last-hour behavior and prior-NY position bucket are exact filters. Provided numeric features are printed range/AVR, U/AVR, D/AVR, position inside prior NY range and position inside the running range.

Distance = average of |historical feature − entered feature| ÷ 0.25
Sort smallest distance first; ties use most recent date.
Take the requested count, or ALL eligible cases.

Earlier saved checkpoints from today may also contribute their time-specific features and filters. With no numeric inputs, there is no closest-case ranking; a finite count selects the most recent eligible cases. ALL still respects the filters. Comparing counts is a sensitivity check, not an independent test: the samples overlap. Extra filters can reduce representativeness and sample stability.

Percentile captions

Printed progress is (running high − running low) ÷ AVR. Its percentile is compared with all eligible same-clock cases before closest-case truncation. Ties receive half weight. Being at a high percentile does not establish exhaustion, and being at a low percentile does not guarantee expansion.

Behavior, session shapes and human error

Use the dictionary for exact definitions and up to five real examples per tag. Last-hour behavior uses completed close paths and explicit 0.05/0.08 × AVR thresholds. Workbook session shapes use their own full-session rules, extreme ordering and close location. Similar names do not make these interchangeable. The historical sequence panel uses prior selected session shapes and research year only; it is separate from closest-case numeric matching.

If uncertain, leave a tag unspecified rather than forcing a label. Screenshots can help review a classification but cannot reliably supply exact bar prices, hidden candles or missing timestamps. An image model can also make mistakes. Automated suggestions should show the evidence, allow “uncertain”, and require review before applying them. Numerical classification from a licensed bar feed is preferable where possible.

New York calculation

The NY panel conditions on Asia’s 02:00 close, London’s 08:30 close, optional completed shapes, and research year. Position is measured either against the shared prior NY BBOX or against each session’s own range. It then measures U/D from the 08:30 cutoff through 10:30 or 16:00. The separate shape distribution uses NY 09:30–16:00.

The displayed mean U/D ratios each multiply your frozen overnight AVR in points. For example, 0.55 U and 0.60 D with AVR 2.00 give U 1.10 and D 1.20 points from the 08:30 price. These are historical conditional excursions, not a “true NY AVR.” The panel is not a regression of overnight volatility; it groups days by the selected positions and shapes. Full London inputs are unavailable at 03:00 or 05:00, so selecting them then is a hypothetical scenario.

Next-hour forecasting versus ATR

ATR averages true bar ranges and is a useful recent-volatility baseline. It does not directly predict the separate highest/lowest prices over a future multi-bar window. Multiplying a five-minute ATR by 12 is not automatically a calibrated one-hour range prediction.

The comparison panel shows a chronological evaluation for both requested windows and the removed one-hour alternatives. Each test uses only earlier dates for training and compares identical outcomes. An ATR baseline is calibrated using historical future-excursion/ATR ratios; an AVR baseline uses historical excursion/AVR ratios. The closest-case model uses the five numeric inputs without subjective tags.

All archive years have already been used for research. This retrospective chronological check is not an untouched holdout or evidence of a tradable edge. It does not validate every custom tag combination, execution costs or live feed differences. Keep the two windows as research context; omit the next-hour UI unless prospective testing shows a stable useful advantage over simpler baselines.
