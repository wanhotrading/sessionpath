What do the data say about the hypotheses?

These are separate outcomes. Price can make another extreme and still finish against that direction. “Follow” means the second side broken: selling for D and buying for U.

Does the state at 09:30, 10:00 or 10:30 matter?

Read the last completed close at the checkpoint, then measure the remaining path through 16:00. The overnight high/low stays fixed at 08:30.

Read the checkpoint groups

Outside: the last close remains beyond the overnight edge on the second-break side.

Reclaimed: that edge was breached after 08:30, and the last close is back across it.

Never breached: price has not breached that edge after 08:30. Boundary: the last close equals the edge.

For D, the edge is the 19:30–08:30 low. For U, it is the high. A close above the overnight low by itself does not establish a reversal.

Does the tail event add information?

Each event is compared with five nearest earlier one-sided events in the same direction and event family. Matching uses conditions already known at 08:30. The same future clock window is measured for the event and its controls.

Differences are event minus matched control. Movement differences use the frozen overnight width; rate differences are percentage points. Intervals resample calendar-month blocks. Reused controls are not separately resampled, so uncertainty can be understated. Intervals are unavailable below 10 dates or five months. Matching does not identify news causes or establish a causal effect.

Would an earlier entry have helped?

Compare following the second-break direction at 09:30 with entering at 10:00 or 10:30 on the same eligible dates. The common 16:00 exit and equal 60-minute holding periods answer different timing questions.

A positive difference favours the later entry; a negative difference favours 09:30. Returns are signed movement divided by the overnight width, before costs. Paired comparisons use identical dates within each row. An interval crossing zero is compatible with either timing choice.

Can the target arrive before the stop?

Enter at the checkpoint open and follow or fade the second-break direction. Test equal target and stop distances of 25% or 50% of the frozen overnight width, with a 16:00 time exit.

If both levels are hit within one candle, their order is unknown. Bounds assign those cases to the stop first or target first; these are path-order bounds, not confidence intervals. Gross R includes time exits. The optional deduction is a hypothetical total round-trip cost: one CL tick = 0.01 point, one NQ tick = 0.25 point. It does not estimate actual fees or slippage. This is a specified price-path test, not a claim of live profitability.

Definitions and downloadable research

04:30 double breaks and both-orange-box events are analysed separately. The second boundary broken supplies direction. Only events confirmed before 08:30 enter this morning study. A NY orange-box result would be known later and cannot qualify a morning signal.

No news cause has been assigned. Overlapping events can share dates. This research records price behaviour; proving an entry advantage requires later unseen data and realistic execution costs.
